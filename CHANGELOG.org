#+TITLE: DONKEY changelog
#+AUTHOR: Michael A Jones (YardQuit)

Newest first. Each heading is a release tag and the date it carries.

Entries for 1.4.1 and earlier were written after the fact, from the
git history. They say what changed for someone *using* the package
rather than repeating every commit, so a release that was mostly
internal work has a short entry even where the diff was large. The
commits themselves remain the record of why.

* 1.14.1 — 2026-09-18

Banked lines stay visible under =hl-line-mode=. The bank's highlight
and the current-line band covered the same line at the same overlay
priority, and Emacs makes no promise about which of two such overlays
shows — so about half the time the band took the line and the bank you
had just made was invisible.

*Fixed — you will notice these*

- =m l= : a banked line shows its own highlight while =hl-line-mode=
  or =global-hl-line-mode= is on. It used to be a coin flip rather
  than a rule: measured over 200 banks, the band hid the bank 116
  times, and in one real configuration 55 times in 100. Nothing else
  about banking changed — the keys, the counts, the messages and what
  =y=, =d= and =p= spend are all as they were — and a line that is
  both banked and selected still shows the selection.

*Under the hood*

- A bank now sits at overlay priority -25, between the current-line
  band at -50 and the region. Two other highlights that sit in that
  band are drawn under a bank as a result: the current line =gud= draws
  while debugging, which is off unless you set
  =gud-highlight-current-line=, and the =next-error= message
  highlight, which is transient.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do.

* 1.14.0 — 2026-09-15

NORMAL state answers every printable key. Thirty-two of them used to
fall through to the major mode, and what they did there depended on
which buffer you were in; a key DONKEY does not bind now does nothing
instead of running whatever the mode put on it. The case that found
it: =idlwave-mode= binds =&= to an anonymous command that types an
ampersand, so =&= typed a character into a buffer you were navigating.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- A key a major mode bound that DONKEY does not now does nothing in
  NORMAL state, and says "is undefined" when you press it. Suppression
  had worked by remapping the typing commands by NAME, and a name
  cannot reach a command that has none, so the guarantee was only ever
  as good as the list. It is answered from under the keymap now rather
  than from a list, so it holds for every printable key and in every
  mode, including the ones nobody has installed.
- To hand a key back to the buffer, unbind it, exactly as before:
  =(keymap-unset donkey-normal-mode-map ";")=. That now works for any
  printable key rather than only the ones DONKEY had bound.
- =M-x donkey-debug-platform= says which kind of buffer the report
  came from — =Insert [S]=, =Insert [E]= or =Insert [I]= — and, where
  something decided it, what decided it. In a Dired buffer it used to
  say "State here: Insert", which is true and tells a bug report
  nothing.

*Under the hood*

- The modeline asks which kind of buffer this is once and remembers
  the answer, where it worked it out on every redisplay. Byte-compiled
  in a Dired buffer the lighter goes from 1.50 µs to 0.38, and in a
  graphical frame from 1.47 to 0.23. The letter and the cursor shape
  come from that one answer now, so the two cannot disagree.
- The test suite's docstrings are held to the package's own spelling
  rules by a guard that renders them. A hundred and one lines were
  wrong in a way that showed only in the rendered text, and nothing
  had ever rendered them.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do, unless you had come to rely on a major mode's key
reaching through NORMAL state in a buffer you write in. If you had,
name it and unbind it:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp

  (with-eval-after-load 'donkey
    (keymap-unset donkey-normal-mode-map "&"))   ; & is the mode's again

#+end_src

Your own bindings are untouched, including a prefix hung on a key
DONKEY does not bind: =(keymap-set donkey-normal-mode-map "s d" ...)=
works as it always did.

* 1.13.0 — 2026-09-14

Two more modes get a section, and the two states that are not NORMAL
stop borrowing INSERT's cursor. =speedbar-mode= takes Dired's shape —
=h= up a directory, =l= into what is under the cursor — and the
=ebrowse= member buffer gets its =l=. The rest is the documentation
catching up with 1.12.0: the install chapter is one method instead of
five, and the chapter on disagreeing with a section had two examples
that quietly took keys away from anyone who pasted them.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- =h= goes up a directory and =l= opens the file or enters the
  directory in a speedbar tree, the pair Dired has. =K= kills the
  buffer on the line, which is where =k= went when DONKEY took it.
  =j= and =k= are plain line motion there: speedbar binds =n= and =p=
  directly instead of remapping =next-line=, so nothing of its own
  reaches =j=. What that costs is the description speedbar echoes as
  you move and the cursor resting on the button glyph — no command
  depends on either, and =I= still asks for the description.
- =l= goes to the member definition in an =ebrowse= member buffer,
  which is what =RET= does there. Forty-one sections ship now, and
  twenty-six of them give =h= or =l= a meaning.
- The cursor in a support mode is a box, where it was INSERT's bar.
  The keys in an =[S]= buffer are NORMAL state's — =h j k l= move and
  nothing you press becomes text — so it looks like NORMAL state
  rather than like typing. =donkey-cursor-support= is the option.
- =donkey-cursor-excluded= sets the cursor in an =[E]= buffer and
  ships as the bar such a buffer already showed, so nothing changes
  there unless you had set =donkey-cursor-insert= yourself. The two
  are independent options: an excluded buffer keeps the shipped bar
  until you set this one to match yours.

*Fixed — you will notice these*

- =setopt donkey-support-modes= no longer warns. The option described
  its commands with a type that only matches what is loaded at that
  moment, and the table names commands from forty packages, so setting
  the option to anything — including back to its own shipped value —
  printed a type warning at every start.
- The README's examples for disagreeing with a section no longer take
  keys away. A section answers with nothing from any later one, so the
  Dired examples, which left out =J= and =K=, left =dired-goto-file=
  and =dired-do-kill-lines= with no key at all for anyone who pasted
  one. The agenda example that takes =SPC= back left out =h= and =l=
  the same way.
- The ~=~ key's row in the binding tables renders as a key again on
  the repository page, and the test that checks those tables against
  the keymap now reads that row, which it never could before.

*Under the hood*

- Installing from a clone is one =use-package= block, which compiles
  the file whenever it is newer than the =.elc= beside it and says in
  the echo area which copy it loaded. The by-hand, straight.el and
  quelpa blocks are gone, along with a third copy of the compile
  recipe.
- What =SPC= costs a support mode was remeasured across all forty-one
  sections rather than the thirty-one the text described: twenty-five
  carry the pager pair, eight a line motion, three put something that
  has another key, and four lose something with no other home, where
  it said two.
- The cursor a buffer asks for is memoized on every input to the
  answer, because it is asked once per command.
- 1,481 tests, up from 1,475.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do. If you had set =donkey-cursor-insert= yourself and want
an excluded buffer to match it, set =donkey-cursor-excluded= to the
same shape. If you would rather tell all four states apart, give
=donkey-cursor-support= and =donkey-cursor-excluded= shapes of their
own.

* 1.12.0 — 2026-09-14

1.11.0 answered "NORMAL state owns the alphabet in a buffer a program
made" by stepping aside altogether: Dired, Ibuffer, Magit and the
tabulated lists went on =donkey-excluded-modes=, which gave the mode
its keys back and took =hjkl= with them. This release is the middle
that was missing, and it comes in two sizes. In a list or a viewer —
Dired, Ibuffer, Occur, the menus, twenty-one modes in all — DONKEY
holds five keys, =h= =j= =k= =l= and =SPC=, and the mode keeps its
whole alphabet. In the eighteen that are text to read — help, Man,
apropos, compilation — it holds about two dozen, because there is
nothing in those buffers to lose. The modeline says =DONKEY[S]= in
both, and thirty-nine modes ship a section.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- =j= and =k= walk the listing and =l= opens what is under the cursor
  in Dired, Ibuffer and the tabulated lists. They came off
  =donkey-excluded-modes= and are support modes instead, so =d=, =m=,
  =x=, =g= and the rest of the mode's alphabet are exactly as they were
  in 1.11.0, and the four motion keys work again. =h= goes up a
  directory in Dired.
- =d= no longer deletes in =help-mode=, =Man-mode=, =occur-mode=,
  =compilation-mode=, =grep-mode= and =org-agenda-mode=, and neither do
  the other editing letters. NORMAL state used to be on in those, which
  meant DONKEY owned the alphabet; they are support modes now. Nothing
  was at risk — the buffers are read-only, so every editing key was
  refused — but the mode's own keys were unreachable, and now they are
  not.
- =SPC= is the leader in a support mode as well as in NORMAL state, and
  it is the same keymap. Anything you have hung under =SPC= reaches
  Dired, help, Man and the rest without being named a second time. The
  mode's own =SPC=, usually =scroll-up-command=, is =C-v= as it always
  was, and =S-SPC= is untouched.
- =g= is a prefix in the eighteen modes that take the reading keys —
  =Man-mode=, =help-mode=, =apropos-mode=, =compilation-mode= and the
  rest — because =g g= and =g e= go there. Where =g= was
  =revert-buffer= or =recompile= the section carries it on =R=.
- =w=, =b=, =y=, =G=, the mark run and =?= work in those eighteen —
  about two dozen keys in all, against five in the other twenty-one.
  They are buffers of text to read and copy, so the reading half of
  NORMAL state is there and nothing that types is. =C-g= still quits:
  it delegates to =keyboard-quit= wherever NORMAL state is off.
- =l= goes to the error in a compilation buffer, into the entry in an
  archive or a tar file, to the occurrence in an Occur buffer, and to
  the reference in an Xref buffer. Twenty-four of the thirty-nine
  sections give =h= or =l= a meaning; where none does they are
  =backward-char= and =forward-char=.

*Added*

- =donkey-support-modes=, the table of sections. A section names the
  mode, optionally a package, and the =(CHARACTER . COMMAND)= pairs
  that say what a key means there. A mode matches by derivation as well
  as by name, and the first section it matches answers, so write a
  specific mode before the general one it derives from.
- =donkey-support-mode-exceptions=, for a buffer that looks like a
  program's but is one you type in. =occur-edit-mode= is the case it
  ships for.
- =donkey-key-packages=, a named list of sequences a section can ask
  for in one word. =prose= is the only one that ships: the reading half
  of NORMAL state, forty-five sequences.

*Under the hood*

- The rule that handed individual letters back to a major mode was
  built, measured and then deleted. Every buffer it would have applied
  to is a support mode, decided by its section, so it found nothing;
  the options it introduced never appeared in a release.
- Options read from =post-command-hook= are coerced rather than
  trusted, a section cannot name a command that types, and the two
  caches that decide a buffer's state are keyed on the same inputs.
- 1,475 tests, up from 1,426.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do. If you put =dired-mode=, =ibuffer-mode= or
=tabulated-list-mode= on =donkey-excluded-modes= yourself, your setting
still wins and those buffers stay =DONKEY[E]=; remove them to get the
four motion keys back. =Info-mode= is still excluded, as it was in
1.11.0.

* 1.11.0 — 2026-09-12

NORMAL state was on in Magit, Dired, Info and the package menu, and
its letters won: =c= was not commit, =d= was not diff, =m= did not
mark. It steps aside in those now, the way it always did in a
terminal. The release came out of driving real keys through 477
packages from ELPA and MELPA and reading what happened.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- Six more modes ship on =donkey-excluded-modes=: =magit-mode= (every
  magit and forge buffer), =dired-mode=, =ibuffer-mode=,
  =git-rebase-mode=, =tabulated-list-mode= (the package menu, the
  buffer menu, and the tabulated UIs other packages build on it) and
  =Info-mode=. In them every key is the mode's, =C-g= means what it
  means in stock Emacs, and the modeline says =DONKEY[E]=. Nothing was
  ever at risk in those buffers — they are read-only, so every editing
  key was refused — but the mode had stopped being usable for the job
  it exists to do. The help-like read-only modes are deliberately left
  alone: =help-mode=, =occur-mode=, =compilation-mode=, =grep-mode=,
  =Man-mode=, =org-agenda-mode=.
- =j= and =k= in Magit and Dired are the mode's own keys again, which
  means =magit-delete-thing= and =dired-do-kill-lines=. Both modes
  remap =next-line=, so while NORMAL state was on those keys moved by
  lines. Two lines per mode take the motion back; the README says
  which.
- The terminals and REPLs =comint-mode= does not cover are on the list
  too: =eat-mode=, =mistty-mode=, =slime-repl-mode=,
  =cider-repl-mode=, =racket-repl-mode=, =haskell-interactive-mode=.
- =C-g= in INSERT state now finishes what a package had open before it
  leaves. With a completion list showing it was the package's own quit
  key, and replacing that with the exit left the list on the screen
  with its keymap live — so =j= walked the candidates in NORMAL state
  and a further key completed a word into the buffer. One press closes
  the list and returns you to NORMAL state.
- NORMAL state's keys answer first whatever loaded last. A minor mode
  defined after DONKEY used to outrank it, so =paredit= typed =; " (
  [=, =haskell-indentation= typed =) , ; ] }=, and =slime= made =SPC=,
  the leader, type a space.
- =U= redoes where =undo-tree= keeps the history. It was =undo-redo=,
  which does nothing once undo-tree owns the undo list, while =u=
  worked — undo-tree remaps =undo= and not =undo-redo=.
- =RET= outside =donkey-editing-modes= asks the buffer what the key
  means at the press rather than at every entry into NORMAL state, so
  a mode that binds it later is answered correctly.
- Five more commands that type a character are refused in NORMAL
  state: =py-electric-comment=, =tagedit-insert-exclamation-mark=,
  =fortran-electric-line-number=, =electric-pascal-hash=,
  =tcl-electric-hash=. Twenty-one in all.
- =wdired= opens in NORMAL state. =C-x C-q= out of an excluded Dired
  used to leave the buffer in INSERT, because that was the state Dired
  had.

*Added*

- =donkey-excluded-mode-exceptions=, empty by default. Read before
  =donkey-excluded-modes= and winning over it, so a mode can stay in
  NORMAL state under an excluded parent. Nothing else could do that:
  =magit-log-mode= is never on the exclusions list — =magit-mode= is,
  and covers it — so removing it there does nothing.
- =M-x donkey-check-bindings= names the entry that decided the state
  in this buffer, and the ancestor where an ancestor is what matched:
  "Normal state is off here: magit-log-mode derives from magit-mode,
  on donkey-excluded-modes". A reader looking for their own mode on
  the list would not have found it.

*Fixed — you will notice these*

- =C-g= after one of DONKEY's prefixes is a quit. =SPC= or =m= or =g=
  and then =C-g= resolved to nothing, which Emacs answers with a bell
  — and a bell is an error inside a keyboard macro, so a macro
  carrying the sequence stopped there.
- =C-g= after =C-x= is Emacs' own diagnostic again. The catch above
  fired after any prefix in its first form.
- A mistyped prefix keeps the selection. =M w w= then =g= and =C-g=
  backed out two levels instead of one, dropping the mark run.
- =C-g= cancels the digraph prompt. It arrived as an ordinary
  character and was spent as a stray key, which ends a complete
  mnemonic by accepting it: =SPC i & ! *= then =C-g= typed a backtick.
- A wrap key handed back to a mode that bound a keyboard macro to it
  is refused instead of signaling.
- A =donkey-input-methods= that is not a list is dropped rather than
  signaling. It signaled inside the =setq= that set it, which in an
  init file takes the rest of the file with it.

*Under the hood*

- Three hooks report rather than signal, and both mark run hooks now
  keep the promise their docstrings made. A hook function that signals
  is dropped for the session.
- Two =declare-function= forms name the library the function actually
  lives in, so =check-declare-file= is clean.
- The README is rewritten around what a reader wants rather than how
  Emacs looks a key up, with a chapter on why those six modes ship
  excluded, one on naming a prefix of your own, a complete
  configuration showing every option with its default, and a note that
  a clone runs from source unless you compile it.
- The tutor teaches wrapping and the digraphs, and the README lists
  all thirteen lessons.
- Neither test harness can leave a transient map armed for whatever
  runs next.

* 1.10.0 — 2026-09-11

Select something in Normal state and press a delimiter: it wraps.
Press it again with the same text selected and it comes off. DONKEY
inserts the pair itself, so the same keys do the same thing in every
buffer whatever else is installed — and it found an old leak on the
way, in which a key a major mode bound to its own insert command
typed in Normal state.

*Added*

- Wrapping a selection. The delimiters are the pairs
  =donkey-mark-pair-delimiters= already knew, so what =m i= can
  select, a key can wrap: =(=, =[=, ={=, =<=, ="=, ='=, =`=, the
  curved quotes, =«=, =‹=, ===, =*=, =~=, =|=, =\=, =/=, =+=, =_=
  and =$=, either half of a pair doing the same thing. Nothing
  between the two characters is touched — no escaping, in any mode —
  and a press whose pair already stands around the selection takes
  it off.
- =donkey-wrap-delimiters=, =all= by default, which means every pair
  the table knows. A pair added to the table is a wrap key too, with
  no second variable to keep in step. Name characters instead and
  only those wrap.
- =donkey-wrap-region-engine= hands the press to Smartparens or
  =electric-pair-mode= instead, and =M-x donkey-toggle-wrap-engine=
  switches between the two, saying which is in force and whether
  anything is pairing in this buffer.
- =SPC i &= wraps as well as inserts: with a selection live it asks
  for an rfc1345 digraph and wraps in the character it names. The
  digraph chart lists the guillemets now, =&<<= and =&>>=.
- =donkey-self-insert-commands=, the commands Normal state refuses
  because they type, with =M-x donkey-refresh-suppressed-commands=.
- DONKEY says what has taken its keys: once at first idle, and from
  =M-x donkey-check-bindings= whenever you like — a key of its own
  that something else runs now, a delimiter that cannot wrap, and,
  asked by hand, the keys another map answers in this buffer.
  =donkey-report-binding-changes= turns the automatic pass off.
- =M-x donkey-debug-platform= opens with a DONKEY section: the
  version, which state is on, the engine, the delimiter counts and
  the same findings.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- Most punctuation is a wrap key now. With no selection those keys do
  nothing in a buffer you can edit, and in a buffer you cannot the
  press goes back to the major mode — so Dired keeps =+=, =(= and
  =<=, Info keeps =[= and =]=, a help buffer keeps =<=.
- =:= and =>= are the two exceptions: they run DONKEY commands of
  their own and go on running them. Asked by hand, DONKEY says so and
  names the half that does wrap.
- =,= =-= =;= answered nothing at all, silently, where every other
  key in the map says "X is undefined". They answer now. The delete
  keys stay silent on purpose.
- The delimiter typed after =m i= or =m a= acts at once. It used to
  be swallowed, with a message saying to press it again.
- While a pairing package wraps, =all= means the six characters such
  a package pairs rather than every pair in the table, because a
  package handed one it does not pair inserts a single character and
  no pair at all.

*Fixed — you will notice these*

- =|= A key a major mode binds DIRECTLY to a command that types —
  =org-force-self-insert= on =|=, cc-mode's electrics on =#=, =*= and
  =/=, AUCTeX's on =$=, =\= and =^= — typed in Normal state.
  Suppression works by remapping =self-insert-command=, which such a
  command never reaches. They are refused by name now.
- =#= A pair added to =donkey-mark-pair-delimiters= in an init file
  never got its key: the keys were claimed when the file loaded and
  an init file adds its pairs afterwards. They are claimed once more
  at the first idle moment, so one line of configuration is the whole
  of it.
- =D= A key your buffer REMAPS was reported as a key you had lost.
  Org remaps =kill-line= to =org-kill-line=, so DONKEY's =D= runs
  org's version — nothing was taken, and the line says so.

*Under the hood*

- The pair table is read as a defcustom rather than trusted: a
  mistyped entry is skipped instead of signalling from a timer, from
  =setopt=, or at the moment of a press.
- A wrap key let go of gets back what it held rather than being
  unbound, an unbound key being the one kind that falls through to
  the major mode.
- The README says what each pairing engine does to a selection that
  already sits inside that delimiter, measured in two modes with
  =electric-pair-mode= and with Smartparens.
- 1,380 tests, from 1,319, driven through the command loop in four
  frame shapes.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do. Keys that used to do nothing in Normal state now wrap
when something is selected, and answer with "X is undefined" when
nothing is. A key you had bound yourself is never taken: the claim
takes only keys that run nothing, and =M-x donkey-check-bindings=
names anything it left alone.

* 1.9.2 — 2026-09-09

=C-\= says what it did.  Emacs switches an input method without a
word, and its one sign is a mode-line field that a custom mode line
need not carry, so the key can look dead; DONKEY now names the
method in the echo area.

*Changed — you will notice these*

- Switching an input method says so.  =C-\= turning =rfc1345= on in
  Insert state says "rfc1345 on", turning it off says "rfc1345 off",
  and pressing it in Normal state, where DONKEY puts the method away
  until you return to Insert, says "rfc1345 on when you enter INSERT
  state".  The name is the method's own, as =C-h I= spells it; the
  =SPC i= keys go on using the label you gave them in
  =donkey-input-methods=.
- What DONKEY does itself still says nothing.  It puts the method
  away on the way into Normal state and brings it back on the way
  out, on every transition, and an echo there would fire on every
  =ESC= and every =i=.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do.  If you would rather have the silence back, DONKEY
has no option for it, but the messages come from the two hooks named
in the Input Method Management section of the README and can be
removed there.

* 1.9.1 — 2026-09-09

Nothing you press behaves differently.  DONKEY now does about a
third of the work between your keystrokes that it did in 1.9.0, and
the README says how to byte-compile a copy you cloned yourself,
which is worth another ninefold.

*Under the hood*

- The cursor update after every command asks first whether anything
  it reads has moved -- the buffer, the selected window, the state's
  cursor setting, and the =cursor-type= another package may have set
  -- and returns when nothing has.  It cost two microseconds a
  command in a graphical frame and now costs one.
- The position ring reuses the marker it drops instead of making a
  new one and copying the list.  Recording a move cost 5.8
  microseconds at the default ring size and 21.6 at a hundred; it now
  costs 3.6 and 3.9, and no longer grows with
  =donkey-position-ring-max=.
- The =C-g= interception in Insert state asks whether the key was
  =C-g= before asking what the buffer's mode is, which is the same
  question in a cheaper order: 1.7 microseconds a keystroke down to
  0.3.
- Together: everything DONKEY does between two keystrokes fell from
  8.7 microseconds to 6.5 read from source, and from 3.1 to 1.0
  byte-compiled.  A test now recounts the five functions DONKEY puts
  on the command hooks, so a sixth is a deliberate decision about
  what a keystroke costs.
- The README's Installation section gains "Byte-compiling a manual
  install": the plain load and the =use-package= clone both run the
  interpreted source, where every DONKEY function costs about nine
  times what the compiled one does.  =straight.el=, =quelpa= and any
  package archive compile it for you; a clone does not.  The section
  gives a form that rebuilds when the source is newer and never loads
  a stale compiled file, and says to keep a symlink rather than a
  hard link when tracking the repository.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do.  Every key does exactly what it did in 1.9.0, in a
terminal and in a graphical frame, which is what the tests and the
live runs in both were checking.

* 1.9.0 — 2026-09-09

A leader key, and the input methods under it. =SPC= in Normal state
is now a prefix for keys of your own, with DONKEY's first prefix
under it: =SPC i= chooses an input method, turns one off, or types a
single digraph.

*Added*

- =SPC= in Normal state is the leader, =donkey-leader-map=, kept for
  your own keys. Write a binding as =(NAME . COMMAND)= and the name
  travels with it: =?= shows it beside the command and which-key,
  part of Emacs since 30, shows it in its popup, with nothing to
  install.
- =SPC i= is the input method prefix. =SPC i .= turns on the rfc1345
  digraphs, =SPC i -= turns the input method off for good, and
  =SPC i &= asks for one digraph's two keys and inserts the character
  with no method turned on, from Normal state too; a count repeats
  it. A method chosen in Normal state waits for Insert state, and the
  echo area says so.
- =donkey-input-methods= adds your own languages under =SPC i=, one
  =(KEY LABEL METHOD)= each, as commands named
  =donkey-input-method-LABEL=; a plain =setq= takes effect at once,

* 1.7.0 — 2026-09-06

A round of live function tests, driven with real keys in a terminal
frame and a graphical one, followed by a review of everything since
1.6.0 and the decisions it raised. Four keys read a count they used to
ignore, four mark keys reach the other way from a gap, =RET= follows a
Markdown link, and a dozen refusals now leave things exactly as they
found them. Minor rather than patch because keys select and open
something different than they did.

*Changed*

- =o= and =O= take a count. =C-u 3 o= opens the usual line under the
  cursor's line and two empty lines beyond it; =C-u 3 O= the usual
  line above and two empty lines above that. The cursor stays on the
  line a bare press opens, only that line is indented, and a count
  below 1 opens one. =i=, =a=, =I= and =A= still ignore a count and
  =c= keeps its own.
- =V= takes a count. =C-u 3 V= selects three rows anchored on the
  cursor's line, exactly what =V J J= selects, and =J= and =K= grow or
  shrink it from that anchor; =C-u -2 V= selects upward. A counted
  press always starts a fresh selection, even over a live session —
  only the bare press cancels. Zero is a bare press. =M= and =m v=
  still ignore a count.
- A count of zero on =m w=, =m W= and =m p= marks one object, as a
  bare press does, the way =m s= always read it. =C-u 0 m w= used to
  mark an empty region and say "Word marked". Negative counts keep
  reaching backward.
- Every mark key has a side. From the space between two words =m w=
  marks the word *ahead* and =m b= the word *behind*, so from one gap
  the two keys are the two words on either side of it — and =M=, which
  is =m w=, takes the word ahead too. =m W= and =m B=, =m s= and
  =m S=, =m p= and =m P= divide their gaps the same way. The eight
  used to agree on the object behind, which made =m w= and =m b= from
  the same space mark the same word. Where the preferred side is empty
  the other answers: the end of a buffer gives the forward keys the
  last object and the start gives the backward keys the first. On an
  object itself nothing changes.
- =g j= joins the selected lines into one. =V J J g j= makes three
  lines one and leaves the fourth; a selection that ends at the start
  of a line leaves that line out; a selection inside a single line
  joins it with the next; a rectangle's rows are lines like any other
  selection's. The selection used to be ignored. Without one, =g j=
  pulls the next line up as before, and a count still says how many.
- =RET= in =markdown-mode= and =gfm-mode= follows the link at point
  through markdown-mode's own =markdown-follow-thing-at-point= and does
  nothing elsewhere on the page. Markdown used to go through the Org
  rules: with Org loaded every press left three lines in =*Warnings*=
  and a task-list item signaled about Org's tab width; without it RET
  did nothing at all, and an inline link was followed in neither case.
  The default link rule is Org's command alone now.
- =.= is a mark run key. =M w .= is three words and =M w . .= four, a
  count carries, and each =.= is one step for =u= to take back. The
  key grew the selection before but ended the mode with it.
- =J= and =K= inside a rectangle move as =j= and =k= do there, keeping
  the column, so the block grows or shrinks by a row. They fell through
  to a plain line motion that landed on column 0 and collapsed the
  block.

*Fixed*

- A wrap key no longer ends a keyboard macro recording. =v w (= while
  F3 was recording stopped it silently, because the way back to Normal
  was =C-g='s, which stops a recording on purpose. Wrapping is not an
  abort.
- A wrap key refuses read-only text before it enters Insert state, at
  every place the wrap would insert, and the selection survives. Under
  =electric-pair-mode= with the selection's last character read-only,
  =m w (= used to leave a stray =(= behind and drop the selection; a
  read-only buffer already refused cleanly since 1.6.0 and still does.
- A bank is whole lines however the text has moved. =m l g j y= copied
  four characters under "Copied 1 line", and =d= removed them and left
  the rest of the line standing; =D= at the end of a banked line and
  =M-^= from below did the same. Banks are read back as the whole lines
  their overlays touch, two banked lines joined into one count as one,
  and the bank key, =m u= and =m U= find a bank that no longer starts
  at its line's start.
- A refused =m i=, =m a=, =m I= or =m A= changes nothing. A =V= session
  or a rectangle kept its highlight but lost its kind when the search
  found no pair, so the =d= that followed took the text as a plain
  region — the line without its newline, the block as one span. The
  key typed next is left alone too: with the cursor on the paren of an
  unbalanced =(abc=, a refused =m i= used to eat the =(= typed to wrap
  what it had kept.
- =m p= on the first character of a paragraph marks *that* paragraph.
  Under a separator line holding spaces or a tab it marked the one
  above; the start is found forward-then-back now, as =mark-paragraph=
  finds it. In Org, =m p= on a headline marks the body under it, which
  is what Emacs's own =mark-paragraph= gives there; it used to mark the
  previous section's body.
- =m i ( m i (= grows the selection a level and inserts nothing. The
  repeat never reads a delimiter, so the paren typed after it out of
  habit ran as a wrap. Every press that marks without stopping at the
  prompt now protects exactly one delimiter press.
- =V= on an empty line acts on the line. =d= took the newline off the
  kill ring, =c= joined the next line up, and =y= saved by count past
  the line; the session's empty region did not read as a selection.
- A new selection forgets the visual-line anchor. =V m w d= removed the
  whole line, =V v h h y= copied it, and =V m w V= said "canceled"
  where a fresh session should have started.
- A wrap key refused in a read-only buffer keeps the selection, and a
  count on a wrap key is ignored: since Emacs 31 =electric-pair-mode=
  reads a live count for itself and deleted characters never typed.
- =:= refuses what is not a line number. =1e999= reads as an infinite
  float, and rounding it signaled a raw arithmetic overflow; it is a
  =user-error= naming the input now, with the cursor left put.
- The =m i= / =m a= refusal names an unprintable key: =RET=, =SPC=,
  =TAB=, =DEL= and a Meta key used to wrap the message, show an empty
  pair of quotes, or signal about a character code.
- =RET= dispatches in modes derived from =org-mode=, such as
  =org-journal-mode=, where it used to do nothing.
- The =*org-scratch*= header quotes its save key as a reference rather
  than between two closing quotes.

*Internal*

- Two tests relied on ambient command state and were caught by the
  pinned shuffle seeds when new tests shifted the order; both bind what
  they depend on now.
- The cursor shape is left alone under =--batch=, where the escape
  sequences went to the test log and cost two fifths of the suite's
  running time.
- The README's table of contents points at anchors GitHub can follow,
  and a run of docstrings that rendered with mismatched quotes render
  matched.

*Upgrading*

Nothing to do. If you had learned that =m w= from a space marks the
word behind, or that =C-u 3 o= opens one line, those are the two
habits that change; every other key that reads differently reads so
only where it used to refuse or mark nothing.

* 1.6.0 — 2026-09-05

Mark run mode was released in 1.5.0 with the word =M= marks treated as
a *head start* — something already selected when the first letter
arrived, so that the letter still marked afresh. It is the run's first
press now. That is a minor version rather than a patch because every
=M= sequence 1.5.0 documented shifts by one press, and because two
mark keys select something different than they did.

*Changed*

- =M= *is* =m w=, and each letter after it is one more =m=-prefixed
  press. =M w= is two words, =M b= the word and the one before it,
  =M s= the word grown to the end of its sentence, =M C-u 3 w= four
  words, and =M w b= is what =m w m w m b= selects. Every sequence in
  the 1.5.0 notes counts one press further along: =M w w b= is now
  =M w b=, =M w p= is =M p=, and =M w g h g l= is =M g h g l=.
- =J= and =K= reach an end apiece from the word =M= holds. =M J= is
  the word grown to the end of its line — the whole line when the
  word is the line's first — and =M K= reaches back to the line's
  start. =M J K= is the whole line from anywhere, in either order, so
  =M J K d= removes the line outright where =M J d= does it only from
  the first word. =V= then =M= adopts a whole line, and where =M=
  found no word to mark either key still marks the current one fresh.
- =M= reaches the way =m w= reaches. From the gap between two words it
  marks the word *behind*, where it used to arrive empty — the two
  keys had no reading under which they should differ. The mode still
  starts empty where =m w= itself refuses: a buffer with no word in it
  at all.
- =m W= marks by *symbol*, not by sexp. In the gap of a key sequence
  quoted in prose it refused with a name directly behind it; on the
  name it took the quote along; behind =#'bar= it skipped to the
  symbol before. Both ends shed punctuation syntax now, while ='=,
  =`= and =#= stay with the form they prefix, so ='bar= still marks
  as ='bar=.
- A negative count on =m w= no longer takes the separator with it, and
  =m W= with one no longer reaches past the point it counted from —
  asked for the three symbols before a word, it used to mark the words
  it was counting away from. Both take their far end from where the
  object behind point ends, point at the start.

*Added*

- Closing delimiters are accepted at the =m i= / =m a= prompt: =m i )=
  means what =m i (= means, =m a ]= what =m a [= does. The cursor
  sitting on either half was already understood; the typed key is now
  too.
- The =m i= / =m a= prompt names =donkey-mark-pair-delimiters= instead
  of reciting it — nineteen characters that grow with every pair you
  add. =C-h v= on the variable shows the pairs in force with your own
  marked as customized, and the README lists the defaults.

*Fixed*

- =m i (= no longer edits the buffer. With the cursor on a delimiter
  the pair is taken from the buffer and the command is finished, so
  the =(= typed as the third key ran as a command of its own — every
  delimiter is bound to =donkey-wrap-region=, which *inserts*. It
  turned =call(alpha) end= into =call((alpha) end=, silently, from a
  sequence meant to select. Both halves of every pair, both commands;
  found by testing one rule — a selection key may not modify the
  buffer — over 7840 key sequences. The press is swallowed once, so
  pressing the delimiter a second time still wraps.
- A refused mark puts the cursor back. =m w=, =m W=, =m s= and =m p=
  and their backward companions step point about while they search and
  reported from wherever the search gave up: on a buffer of whitespace
  all four sentence and paragraph keys came to rest at position 1 from
  a press at 3, and =m W= in =help-mode= reported nine characters from
  where it was pressed.
- =m i= / =m a= on an empty pair leaves the cursor where it was rather
  than one character along.
- The mark run reminder is not painted over a count as it is typed:
  the keys of =C-u 3= arrive under the name of the command before
  them, and the echo area is the keystroke echo's while a count is
  being entered.
- =donkey-mode= disabled in a buffer leaves nothing behind. Three
  pieces dated to the first release survived the sweep: a
  =pre-command-hook= entry, a flag and a timer.

*Internal*

- Test harnesses no longer reach the machine's clipboard. Nothing in
  batch or a terminal frame can show it, but in a graphical frame
  =yank= took the desktop selection over the test's own kill ring, and
  a run left its last kill on the clipboard. The three bindings that
  keep a test off it are one shared list now.

* 1.1.0 — 2026-07-27

The largest release before 1.5.0.

- *Banked line selection* — =m l= to bank, =m u= / =m U= to unbank —
  for non-contiguous copy and delete, with one rule settling bank
  versus rectangle in all three commands.
- Counts are honoured throughout donkey's own commands, including
  levels for =m i= / =m a= and a count for =J= / =K=.
- Sentence marking marks the sentence *under* the cursor, marks the
  one coming from the gap after a sentence, and reports cleanly when
  there is none.
- Wrapping works with =electric-pair-mode= and over rectangles.
- =G= is bound to =end-of-buffer=; the bindings screen is grouped by
  prefix as documented.
- Banking is linear rather than quadratic, and banked overlays
  evaporate so a bank cannot outlive its text.
- A CI workflow runs the suite, byte-compile, checkdoc and
  package-lint.
- Many =donkey-mark-inner= / =donkey-mark-outer= corrections: nested
  same-type delimiters, closing delimiters, curly quotes, and no
  silent jump when there is no match.
